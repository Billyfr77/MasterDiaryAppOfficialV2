The spatial intelligence container of the Neural Operating System
A Zone represents a physical or logical area of the job site.
It is the anchor for:

cost aggregation

crew performance

equipment utilisation

material usage

spatial causality

simulation

intervention

cross‑job learning

Zones are the geospatial backbone of your Neural OS.

🎨 1. HOW A ZONE SHOULD LOOK
A Zone must visually communicate:

boundaries

cost

drift

activity

risk

density

Here’s the ideal visual layout.

1.1 Zone Shape
A Zone is a polygon or rectangular region on the canvas.

It should have:

a subtle fill colour

a bold outline

a label

a high‑contrast OCR tag (e.g., ZN‑03)

Zones should be visually distinct but not overpowering.

1.2 Zone Header
Displayed at the top-left of the zone:

Zone Name (e.g., “Zone A – North Wall”)

Status Badge

Green → On track

Amber → At risk

Red → Drifting

OCR Tag

Cost Summary

Drift Summary

1.3 Zone Heatmap Overlay
Zones should support a heatmap mode:

Red → high cost / high drift

Yellow → moderate drift

Green → stable

Blue → under‑utilised

This gives instant spatial awareness.

1.4 Node Density Indicators
Small dots or shading to show:

how many nodes are inside

how active the zone is

how congested the zone is

2.3 Aggregate Drift
